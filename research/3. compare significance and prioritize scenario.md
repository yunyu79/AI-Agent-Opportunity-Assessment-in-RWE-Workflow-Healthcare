# Pain Point Definitions

Definition 1 - **Manual processing burden:** Completing a task requires extensive manual operations, reading, or expert judgment, resulting in substantial human time investment.        

Definition 2 - **Repetitive work:** The same or similar information processing and operations are performed repeatedly.

Definition 3 - **Coordination and waiting:** Tasks depend on clarification, handoffs, confirmation, or approval across individuals or teams, creating additional communication effort and waiting time that extend delivery timelines.

Definition 4 - **Errors and rework:** Errors, omissions, or inconsistencies in data, logic, code, or outputs require additional checks, potentially including tracing back through the processes and results of multiple upstream steps.

Definition 5 - **Difficulty adapting and reusing existing work:** In new projects, existing logic, code, or deliverables cannot be readily adapted or reused, requiring substantial modification, adaptation, and revalidation.

## Project-Defined Rating Scales

These are project scoring conventions, not published industry standards. The assigned scores above are hypotheses requiring validation.

| Level | Task frequency, per team | Repetitive operation person-hours / total person-hours | Error rate: confirmed defective units / inspected units | Collaboration Time: active collaboration person-hours / total active person-hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Less than 1 task/month | Below 20% | Below 1% | Below 5% |
| 2 | 1–3 tasks/month | 20% to below 40% | 1% to below 3% | 5% to below 10% |
| 3 | 4–9 tasks/month | 40% to below 60% | 3% to below 5% | 10% to below 20% |
| 4 | 10–19 tasks/month | 60% to below 80% | 5% to below 10% | 20% to below 35% |
| 5 | At least 20 tasks/month | 80%–100% | 10%–100% | 35%–100% |

**Error rate:** Preserve the existing table entries as percentages, not ratings. For example, 5% maps to level 4, 3% to level 3, and 2% to level 2. Count each defective inspected unit once, even if it contains multiple defects. Define the inspected unit (e.g., screening decision, mapping, QC decision) before measurement and use the same denominator and review stage before and after AI. Disagreement is not a confirmed error without adjudication. Severity must be recorded separately; a low rate of critical errors can still be unacceptable.

**Collaboration Time:** This level measures the share of active labor spent on clarification, meetings, handoffs, confirmation and approvals. A one-hour meeting with three participants contributes three person-hours. Collaboration labor is part of total labor, not an additional cost to add again. Record elapsed approval/waiting days separately; calendar waiting is not convertible directly to person-hours. Existing collaboration levels remain unvalidated estimates until confirmed by time logs or structured interviews. These proposed thresholds are project conventions.

**Score calculation**

Let normalized weights sum to 1. For each scenario:

- **Labor dimension:** $(manual hours − AI-assisted hours) × 100 / 320$. Calculating absolute time savings matters in the overall ranking Use 320 instead of each original hour to account for why 
- **Error dimension:** $(manual error percentage − AI-assisted error percentage) × 100 / 5 $. 
- **Repetition dimension:** $(manual repetition level − AI repetition level) × 100 / 4$.
- **Frequency dimension:** $(baseline frequency level − 1) × 100 / 4$.
- **Collaboration dimension:** $(manual collaboration level − AI collaboration level) × 100 / 4$.
- **Overall score:** Sum of normalized weight × dimension score.

The fixed 320-person-hour and 5-percentage-point anchors reflect the largest baseline values in the current input snapshot; they are project scaling choices, not industry standards. A value exceeding an anchor saturates that dimension; revisit the anchors before using substantially different workloads. Fixed anchors keep slider comparisons stable. Treating ordinal level steps as equally spaced is a modeling assumption.

Negative improvements remain negative. Missing or invalid inputs exclude a scenario instead of silently filling in zero. All-zero weights produce no ranking. An increased post-AI error rate triggers a **quality HOLD** flag; it cannot be the tool's recommended candidate regardless of its numerical score. Equal error rates do not establish quality equivalence. Tied scores share a rank.

### Comparability and validation checks

- Preserve the existing source values and R/E/C labels; the linked references and their applicability have not been independently revalidated in this update. **C** means an arithmetic conversion, not an independent measurement.
- Task hours must refer to the same workload and quality standard on both sides. The original headers say project versus task; the score assumes those boundaries match and must be validated.
- W09 is scored as screening only, consistent with its unit-time notes. The AI row's scenario name also mentions evidence extraction; that naming mismatch does not justify assuming extraction is included in the measured hours.
- W04's 100-field scope and per-field minutes do not reconcile with all batch-hour entries. The ranking uses task hours only; resolve this discrepancy before business claims.
- Error percentages from different kinds of inspected units are only heuristic cross-scenario comparisons. Confirm denominators, review stages and severity with experts.
- Frequency and repetitive/collaboration levels are estimates. They are not actual annual volumes or measured fractions.
- Labor savings, repetition reduction and collaboration reduction may be correlated. Their weights represent different decision preferences; they are not independent financial benefits and must not be summed as ROI.
- Sensitivity examples: with labor savings at 100% weight, W01 leads; with frequency or collaboration reduction at 100%, W05 leads. This ranking is preference-sensitive.
- Validation script: run `node test_scoring.cjs` from the research directory. It checks the HTML's embedded scoring logic, scale boundaries, ties, missing inputs, negative gains, zero weights and slider-controller behavior.


## Needs Validation / Industry Expert Interview

- **W01 — RWE scientists and epidemiologists:** Confirm protocol complexity, role allocation, preparation/drafting/discussion/review hours, incoming task volume, repetitive work and protocol defect definitions.
- **W09 — Evidence synthesis researchers:** Confirm the screening and extraction batch, independent review requirements, adjudication time, error denominators, task volume and transferability of S1.
- **W04 — Data engineers and clinical informatics experts:** Fix source complexity, field count, coding systems and data quality; measure mapping, cleaning, exception handling and review effort.
- **W05 — Data analysts and QA reviewers:** Confirm exception severity, investigation/resolution scope, false positives, verification effort and task frequency.
- **W07 — Epidemiologists, programmers and clinicians:** Specify endpoint type (e.g., OS or rwPFS), data sources, observation windows and reference standard; measure implementation and clinical review time.
- Validate estimated error rates, role counts, frequency and repetitive labor scores with task records or structured interviews. Validate AI time savings and residual quality in a matched pilot; expert opinion alone does not establish realized gains.
