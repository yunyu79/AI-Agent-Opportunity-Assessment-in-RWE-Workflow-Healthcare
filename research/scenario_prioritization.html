Scoring method and interpretation

Score = Σ(normalized weight × dimension score). Labor gain = (baseline hours − AI hours)/320 × 100; error gain = (baseline error% − AI error%)/5 percentage points × 100. Both are clipped to −100…100. Repetition and collaboration gains = (baseline level − AI level)/4 × 100. Frequency = (baseline level − 1)/4 × 100.

The fixed 320-hour and 5-percentage-point anchors are project conventions tied to this input snapshot, not industry benchmarks. Negative gains stay negative. The 1–5 levels are ordinal approximations; equal spacing is a decision-model assumption. Collaboration hours are a subset of total labor; its small separate weight reflects handoff friction and may overlap with labor savings. Frequency is not multiplied by labor hours, so this is not an annual-value calculation.

Unit processing times are shown in the Markdown source but are not independently weighted: protocols, articles, fields and exceptions have incompatible units, and reusing per-unit time alongside task hours would duplicate the time signal. W09 is scored as screening only. The original AI row mentions extraction; confirm matching scope before accepting its apparent gain. W04 source-field minutes also do not reconcile with the batch hours. Source labels are carried from the document and have not been independently verified.

Default rationale: labor savings 35%, error reduction 25%, repetitive work 15%, task frequency 15%, collaboration 10%. Quality improvement is important, but error severity is not captured by raw rates. Increases in errors trigger HOLD rather than an automatic recommendation. Equal error rates do not prove equivalent safety.
